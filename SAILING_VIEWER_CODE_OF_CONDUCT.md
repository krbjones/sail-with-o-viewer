# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

The Sail With O Viewer project is an open-source project for recreational sailors, racing sailors, coaches, developers, and sailing enthusiasts who want to learn from their time on the water.

We are committed to making participation in this project a welcoming, respectful, constructive, and harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, background, nationality, race, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, religion, experience level, sailing ability, technical ability, or any other personal characteristic.

We believe that sailing is a lifelong learning activity, and the same should be true of this project. Everyone should feel comfortable asking questions, sharing ideas, reporting problems, experimenting with improvements, and contributing at whatever level they can.

## Our Community

Sail With O Viewer exists to help people get more from their sailing.

The project aims to provide useful tools for reviewing races, exploring sailing tracks, understanding what happened on the water, and identifying opportunities to improve future performance. Recreational sailors, racing sailors, coaches, instructors, and developers may all approach the application from different perspectives. Those perspectives are valuable.

We encourage contributors to:

* Share knowledge and experience generously.
* Ask questions, including basic questions.
* Welcome newcomers and help them get started.
* Give constructive feedback on ideas, code, documentation, and proposed changes.
* Respect that sailing techniques, racing experience, boats, equipment, and conditions vary.
* Discuss technical and sailing-related disagreements respectfully.
* Share ideas for improving the application and its usefulness to sailors.
* Fork, modify, experiment with, and build upon the project in accordance with its license.
* Submit improvements, bug fixes, documentation, analysis tools, visualizations, and new ideas through the project's normal contribution process.
* Give credit where credit is due.
* Be open to learning from people with different sailing and technical backgrounds.

A contribution does not need to be perfect to be valuable. A question, bug report, proposed feature, experiment, or partially completed improvement can help move the project forward.

## Expected Behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive community include:

* Using welcoming and inclusive language.
* Treating other participants with respect and patience.
* Assuming good intentions while remaining open to feedback when something has gone wrong.
* Giving and accepting constructive criticism gracefully.
* Focusing criticism on ideas, implementations, or results rather than individuals.
* Explaining technical or sailing concepts when doing so would help others learn.
* Respecting different levels of sailing and programming experience.
* Respecting different opinions about sailing strategy, tactics, technology, and software design.
* Helping others find solutions rather than simply pointing out problems.
* Being considerate of people's time and circumstances.
* Keeping discussions relevant to the project and its goals.

Healthy disagreement is welcome. Debate about code, design decisions, sailing analysis, racing strategy, or proposed features can make the project better. The goal is to challenge ideas without attacking the people who hold them.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

* Harassment, intimidation, threats, or personal attacks.
* Discriminatory, hateful, or derogatory language or imagery.
* Trolling or deliberately provoking other participants.
* Sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted sexual attention.
* Publishing or sharing another person's private or personally identifying information without permission.
* Repeated unwanted contact or attention.
* Deliberately disrupting project discussions or collaboration.
* Misrepresenting another person's statements or contributions in order to attack or discredit them.
* Personal attacks based on someone's sailing ability, technical experience, background, or level of participation.
* Any other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate, abusive, or harmful in an open-source community.

## Project and Sailing Discussions

Sailing is inherently a subject where reasonable people can disagree. Different boats, crews, conditions, racing rules, sailing locations, and levels of experience can produce very different conclusions.

Disagreement about sailing technique or race analysis is therefore not, by itself, a Code of Conduct violation.

Participants should nevertheless keep these discussions constructive. When presenting an analysis or recommendation, distinguish personal experience and opinion from established facts where practical, and be willing to reconsider conclusions when new information becomes available.

The application is a tool for analysis and learning. It should not be represented as a substitute for sound judgment, appropriate sailing knowledge, navigation practices, racing rules, or decisions made by a skipper or crew on the water.

## Contributing to the Project

Sail With O Viewer is intended to benefit from contributions from the wider sailing and open-source communities.

You are encouraged to:

* Fork the repository and experiment with improvements.
* Create branches for new features or ideas.
* Submit pull requests.
* Report bugs and suggest improvements.
* Improve documentation and examples.
* Propose new ways of visualizing or analyzing sailing data.
* Develop features that may help sailors and coaches learn from race data.
* Adapt the application for new use cases, boat types, data sources, or sailing communities.
* Share your experience using the application.

Not every contribution will be accepted, and maintainers may have to make difficult decisions about scope, design, compatibility, or project direction. A declined contribution is not a rejection of the contributor. We encourage contributors to discuss alternatives and continue improving ideas together.

## Data and Privacy

When sharing sailing tracks, screenshots, examples, or other data, contributors should take reasonable care not to expose another person's private information without permission.

In particular, consider whether uploaded GPS tracks or screenshots contain personally identifying information, home addresses, private sailing locations, names, contact information, or other information that should not be made public.

If you discover sensitive information in the repository, an issue, or another project channel, please avoid redistributing it and contact the maintainers so that it can be addressed appropriately.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all project spaces, including:

* The GitHub repository.
* Issues and pull requests.
* Discussions and comments.
* Project documentation and examples.
* Code reviews and commits.
* Other online spaces used for project collaboration.

It also applies when an individual is representing the Sail With O Viewer project or its community in other public spaces.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing the standards described in this Code of Conduct.

Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject comments, issues, commits, pull requests, documentation, or other contributions that violate this Code of Conduct.

Depending on the circumstances and severity of a violation, maintainers may:

1. Ask the participant to stop the behavior or correct the contribution.
2. Remove or edit offending content.
3. Issue a warning.
4. Temporarily restrict participation in project spaces.
5. Permanently restrict participation or contribution to the project.
6. Take other reasonable action necessary to protect the community.

Enforcement decisions will be made with the goal of being fair, proportionate, and consistent.

Serious or repeated violations may result in immediate restriction or removal from the project.

## Reporting Problems

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, please report it to the project maintainer through the contact information or private communication channel listed in the repository.

When possible, include:

* A description of what happened.
* When and where it happened.
* Links to relevant issues, pull requests, comments, or other material.
* Any other information that may help the maintainers understand the situation.

Please do not publicly post sensitive personal information as part of a report.

Reports will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible. The project will not tolerate retaliation against someone who makes a good-faith report or participates in an investigation.

If there is an immediate threat to someone's safety, contact the appropriate emergency or local authorities rather than relying on the project maintainers to resolve the situation.

## Our Goal

The goal of Sail With O Viewer is simple: help people get better at sailing by making it easier to understand what happened during a race or sailing session.

A healthy open-source community can make that tool substantially better than any one person could make it alone. Sailors bring knowledge of boats, tactics, weather, racing, and life on the water. Coaches bring experience in analysis and instruction. Developers bring software and data expertise. New users bring fresh perspectives and ideas.

We welcome all of these contributions.

**Build something useful. Share what you learn. Help others improve. And enjoy the sailing.**
